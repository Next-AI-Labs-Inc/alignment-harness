You are given notes about one person, written from many of their past Claude Code sessions (one JSON note per session: what they were after, corrections they made to an agent, leans, decisions, vocabulary, with each session's id and date). Write the map a new agent reads before working with this person, so it can orient to their intent the moment they speak.

Write Markdown, and nothing else. No preamble. Use exactly these sections, in this order, each as a "## " heading:

## What they are usually working toward
2-5 sentences on the larger aims that recur across sessions, in their own terms.

## Leans to weigh
The most useful leans, strongest first, grouped by the situation they fire in. One bullet each: the situation, the lean, why, how many sessions show it, and the session ids in brackets, e.g. [s:1a2b3c4d, s:9f8e7d6c]. Where two leans pull against each other, say so in both bullets. 6-20 bullets.

## Corrections they have had to make
The mistakes agents made with them that they had to correct, most repeated first, each with the wrong belief, what they meant, and session ids. These matter most: a future agent can avoid making the person correct it again.

## Their words
Terms and names they use and what they mean by them.

## What I am still unsure of
Open questions about their intent the notes leave unanswered.

Rules:
- The notes speak to the person as "you"; keep that: write the map as if talking to them ("you lean toward …"), so it reads the same to them and to the agent.
- These are leans to weigh, not rules to obey. Never write "always", "never" or "must"; say how strongly the evidence leans and how often it showed up.
- A lean seen in one session is a slight lean; say so. Do not inflate.
- Only use quotation marks for words copied exactly from a note's "quote" field. Otherwise paraphrase without quotation marks.
- Cite session ids as [s:<first 8 characters of the id>].
- Write in plain words a newcomer understands; explain any code name inside the sentence that uses it.
- Stay under 1,200 words.
